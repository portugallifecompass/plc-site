Healthcare in Portugal: What Expats Actually Pay in 2026

Episode 12 guide · Published October 2026

RULES AS OF JULY 2026

Portugal's constitution does not say healthcare is free. It says the National Health Service is “tendencialmente gratuito” — tending towards free. That one word is the whole story, and it is why so much expat content about Portuguese healthcare is either too optimistic or years out of date.

If you live here legally, you are in the system

The Health Basic Law is explicit: foreign nationals resident in Portugal — permanently or temporarily — are beneficiaries of the SNS on the same footing as Portuguese citizens. That covers EU nationals, non-EU nationals, stateless persons and applicants for international protection (Base 21 of Law 95/2019, confirmed by article 4 of the SNS Statute, Decree-Law 52/2022).

Your número de utente — the SNS user number — is free. As a foreign resident you get it at your first visit to a public health unit. For the SNS to actually bear your costs you will need an ID document, a Portuguese NIF, your full address in Portugal, and a valid residence permit.

What you actually pay: one fee, and only one

Here is what most content gets wrong. The 2022 reform abolished user fees across the SNS except in one place. The law now reads: fees are due “apenas nos serviços de urgência hospitalar” — only in hospital emergency departments (Decree-Law 113/2011, article 2, as amended by Decree-Law 37/2022).

No fee for a GP appointment. No fee for a specialist appointment. No fee for tests ordered outside the emergency room. If a video or blog post quotes you a €4.50 consultation fee, it is describing rules that ended in 2022.

Published SNS hospital tables show the emergency fee at €14 at a basic emergency unit, €16 at a medical-surgical emergency and €18 at a top-tier polyvalent emergency, plus capped charges for tests done during the same episode — hospitals publish a €40 cap on those.

The trick almost nobody uses

The emergency fee disappears entirely if the SNS referred you. Call the SNS 24 line — 808 24 24 24 — or go through your health centre first, and there is no fee. The same applies if you are admitted to hospital through the emergency department. It is good clinical practice and it is also, in law, the difference between €18 and nothing.

Whole groups are exempt from any user fee regardless: under-18s, pregnant women, people with a disability of 60% or more, people in proven economic hardship (household income per person up to 1.5 times the social support index — €805.70 a month in 2026), blood and organ donors, asylum seekers and refugees, among others.

Visiting, not moving? Different rules

If you are an EU, EEA, Swiss or UK visitor, your European Health Insurance Card gives you medically necessary state healthcare during a temporary stay, at the same fees locals pay. It does not cover you if you travelled specifically to get treatment, and it does not cover private providers.

EU retirees moving here permanently: the EHIC is not your route. Ask your home country's health institution for the S1 form — the certificate of your right to healthcare when you live outside the country that insures you — and register it at the district Social Security centre for your area. That registration is also how you get your SNS user number.

The insurance confusion, cleared up

Two different things get merged constantly, and the confusion sells policies.

At the visa stage, the regulation requires valid travel insurance covering necessary medical expenses, including urgent medical assistance and possible repatriation (Decree Regulatory 84/2007, article 12(e)). That is a travel policy for the application — not a lifetime health plan.

Once you are a resident, you are an SNS beneficiary by law. Private health insurance in Portugal is voluntary and runs alongside the public system; having it or not having it does not change your legal right to use the SNS.

At the residence-permit stage the general legal conditions centre on means of subsistence, accommodation and, where applicable, social-security registration. A permanent private-insurance obligation is not the general rule once you are covered by the SNS as a resident. Requirements can differ at renewal — check AIMA's current document list for your permit type.

If you are here on a temporary-stay visa rather than a residence permit, do not assume automatic SNS coverage: your travel policy is the safety net until you hold a permit and register.

Consumer-tested examples for a 40-year-old ranged from about €157 a year for hospital-only cover to roughly €550–920 a year for mid-tier plans with outpatient cover.

What people actually buy it for is speed and choice — shorter waits for non-urgent specialists, and picking your own doctor. Not access. Access you already have.

The tax deduction nobody mentions

Health expenses reduce your Portuguese income tax: you can deduct 15% of household health expenses, up to a global cap of €1,000 — and premiums of policies that exclusively cover health risks count towards it (CIRS, article 78.º-C). Expenses charged at the standard VAT rate only count with a medical prescription.

If you don't have a residence permit yet

You are not simply turned away. But since the December 2024 rules, full SNS cost coverage requires an updated registration. Without it the SNS bears the cost only of urgent and vital care and of specific protected categories — maternal and child health, vaccination, minors. In other cases the cost falls on you.

FAQ

Is healthcare free in Portugal?

Not quite. The constitution says the SNS is “tending towards free”. Since 2022 the only user fee left is in hospital emergency departments — €14 to €18 — and even that disappears if the SNS referred you.

Do I need private health insurance to live in Portugal?

No. Legal residents are SNS beneficiaries by law. Travel insurance is required at the visa application stage; that is a different thing from a permanent private health plan.

Can I use my EHIC after moving to Portugal?

No. The EHIC covers temporary stays. If you move here, you register with the SNS — and EU retirees use the S1 form.
