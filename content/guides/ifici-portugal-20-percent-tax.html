The IFICI Explained: Portugal's 20% Tax Regime, Line by Line

Episode 2 guide · Published July 2026

RULES AS OF JULY 2026

The IFICI is Portugal's successor to the NHR: a special 20% rate on net employment and self-employment income (categories A and B), for 10 consecutive years — but only for a narrow list of eligible activities, and only if you pass the personal tests and register on time. This guide goes through the regime line by line, based on Article 58-A of the Tax Benefits Statute (added by Law 82/2023, the 2024 State Budget) and Ordinance 352/2024/1.

What the IFICI gives you

A special 20% rate on net income from categories A (employment) and B (self-employment) earned in eligible activities, for 10 consecutive years. That is the whole benefit — and its boundaries matter as much as the number. Pensions, dividends, rents and capital gains are not category A or B income, so the IFICI does nothing for them. Note also that as a Portuguese tax resident your worldwide income remains within the Portuguese tax net; the IFICI changes the rate on qualifying income, not the scope of taxation.

The personal tests

To qualify you must become Portuguese tax resident under the normal rules (Article 16 CIRS) and not have been Portuguese tax resident in the previous five years. You are excluded if you benefit or ever benefited from the old NHR, if you opted for the former-residents regime (Article 12-A CIRS), or if you have already used the IFICI itself.

The eligible-activity routes

The regime is built around activity routes, each with its own competent entity: teaching and scientific research (FCT); productive investment (AICEP); highly qualified professions (Tax Authority); jobs of national economic relevance (AICEP/IAPMEI); R&D and SIFIDE-linked roles (ANI); and certified startups (Startup Portugal).

The highly-qualified-professions route is the one most applicants ask about. The published list works by CPP occupation codes — directors and executive managers (112, 12, 13), science and engineering specialists (21), ICT specialists (25), physicians (221), university teachers (231), industrial designers (2163.1) — and demands qualifications: either EQF level 8 (doctorate), or level 6 (degree) plus three years of documented experience. For this route, your employer must also qualify — broadly, a company exporting at least 50% of its turnover, or one with relevant investment.

The deadline that catches people

Registration closes on 15 January of the year after you become resident (Ordinance 352/2024/1, Article 2(1)). The Tax Authority makes the outcome of your registration available by 31 March. Assemble your evidence — contracts, degrees, employer declarations — before applying, not after.

Changing jobs, and interruptions

The regime survives a job change if you resume an eligible activity within six months. If the benefit is interrupted, it can be resumed for the remaining years of the original window — the 10-year clock does not restart.

Who should not build a plan on the IFICI

Retirees living on pensions, and anyone whose income is mostly investment income: the IFICI does not cover categories H (pensions), E (capital income) or G (capital gains). If that is your situation, the relevant rules are the ordinary progressive rates plus treaty relief — see how Portugal taxes foreign pensions and the double-taxation treaties guide.

FAQ

Does the IFICI cover pensions or dividends?

No. It applies only to net employment (category A) and self-employment (category B) income from eligible activities.

What is the IFICI registration deadline?

15 January of the year following the one in which you became Portuguese tax resident — set by Ordinance 352/2024/1.

I used the old NHR. Can I now use the IFICI?

No. Anyone who benefits or ever benefited from the NHR is excluded, as is anyone who opted for the Article 12-A former-residents regime or already used the IFICI.
